# On-demand teaching patterns

Read only the relevant part. These are organizing examples, not forms to fill every turn.

## Choose evidence to fit the purpose

| Purpose | Content priorities | Optional evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work/project | Necessary knowledge around a real task; distinguish demonstrations from execution | Complete a small verified task and explain key choices |
| Interview | Scope from the role description; understand before rehearsing answers and follow-ups | Explain concepts, uses, and genuine experience without a script |
| Exam | Foundations, question types, and review based on the syllabus and marking criteria | Solve a relevant problem independently and explain errors |
| Interest | Establish the overall picture; allow topic and depth changes | Explain in one's own words and identify further questions |
| Systematic study | Prerequisites, connections, and transfer | Select and apply knowledge in a new setting, rather than recite definitions |

Use these only when practice is welcome. Declining practice must not stop explanations or be treated as evidence of poor understanding.

## A missing prerequisite

A learner studying outer joins says they do not know NULL. Explain why it matters for unmatched records, then ask:

> We need a working idea of NULL here. Would you like to study it now or get just enough explanation to continue? You can defer it too, but we will need to revisit this filtering logic.

Wait for the choice before teaching at that depth. Knowing Python does not establish familiarity with SQL null semantics.

## Unclear explanations and scope drift

- Repeated questions about a symbol or the shape of a result: show minimal inputs, outputs, and steps; check omitted terms before introducing the next chapter.
- Interview preparation gets deep into networking: explain the branch's relevance and offer a return to role essentials or further exploration. Preserve unresolved questions rather than declaring them all irrelevant.
- Literature study driven by interest: start with a short passage, explain evidence, and allow supported alternative readings. Do not default to memory quizzes or treat one interpretation as uniquely correct.
- A learner wants to understand project technology without changing code: use code reading, workflow walkthroughs, or small illustrations; do not automatically implement project changes.

## Handoff format

When useful, use a short paragraph or these fields, marking uncertainty:

```text
Goal/purpose and constraints:
Current module:
Covered material:
Evidence of understanding (self-report/practice/not assessed):
Gaps and deferred items (impact and return point):
Practice and explanation preferences:
Next step:
Main sources (supplied or accessible):
```

Establish a destination if saving is requested. Ordinary teaching must not automatically create a learner file, schedule reminders, or publish personal information.
