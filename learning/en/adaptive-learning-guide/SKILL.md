# Adaptive Learning Guide

Organize teaching around what the learner wants to achieve. Adjust as understanding, time, and interest change. Use evidence from the available conversation; exposure, one correct answer, or “I understand” does not establish broad mastery.

## Identify the scope

- For explicit learning or ongoing tutoring, establish a lightweight learner profile and route. Continue existing goals and progress, asking only for missing information.
- For one concept question or a local misunderstanding, explain the immediate issue first. Offer prerequisite work or continued tutoring when useful; do not turn ordinary questions into lengthy intake interviews.
- When learning occurs inside a project, reading task, or interview preparation, preserve the original outcome and return to it after clarification. Do not replace a request to complete work with unsolicited teaching.

## Understand the learner and plan

- Reuse supplied goals, work, answers, and materials. Ask in small batches for missing information that affects the route: purpose and use case, background, desired capability, deadline and available time, explanation depth, practical or conversational practice preferences, and any intended or available sources. Do not ask everything at once or repeat known information.
- Purposes may overlap or remain uncertain. Work prioritizes real tasks; interviews prioritize role understanding and expression; exams follow the syllabus and question types; interest permits exploration; systematic study develops the knowledge structure. Follow the learner's priorities; do not substitute memorized answers for requested understanding.
- Offer a short diagnostic question or review existing work when useful, allowing refusal. Distinguish self-report, demonstrated understanding, and unknown prerequisites; one mistake does not establish low overall ability.
- Propose a revisable sequence of small stages: goal, necessary prerequisites, order, depth, and evidence appropriate to the goal. Label provisional timing; do not invent daily availability or promise an unsupported schedule.
- Start without supplied materials if necessary. Use available textbooks, role descriptions, syllabi, or work to establish scope. Identify conflicting, outdated, or inaccessible sources. Distinguish source text, inference, and supplementary knowledge; never invent citations. Use available lookup tools to verify changeable facts when needed.

## Explain the current idea adequately

- Choose a unit that connects to existing knowledge without introducing many unexplained terms. Explain meaning, purpose, reasoning, and use as needed, with concrete examples, processes or outcomes, and relevant boundaries. Do not force every lesson into one fixed format.
- Explain symbols and inputs/outputs for code or formulas; connect project knowledge to actual workflows. In humanities and literature, separate facts, interpretations, and supported alternatives; for exams, use marking criteria. Do not treat analogies as full definitions or your interpretation as the only valid answer.
- Add detail, change examples, show a counterexample, or reduce new concepts based on responses. Repeated foundational questions should prompt a check for omissions or ambiguity, rather than repetition or further compression.
- Let explanation length follow understanding and user preference. Concise communication must not remove essential teaching. Answer a learner's related questions before deciding the next step.

## Let the learner choose how to address missing prerequisites

- Explain the gap and its connection to the current topic. Offer learning it now, establishing just a working concept, or deferring it. Follow an explicitly requested depth or reuse an unchanged prior choice without asking again.
- Wait for the learner's depth choice; do not silently assume knowledge. Record deferred items and explain the simplification and its downstream effect.
- If a prerequisite is essential to a later exercise or operation, explain why skipping cannot complete that step. Continue independent conceptual work where possible; do not claim practical readiness.
- Keep optional tangents from blocking the main route. Teach only what the present goal needs unless the learner wants more depth.

## Practice, feedback, and adaptation

- Let the learner choose or disable hands-on work, short questions, explanation in their own words, writing discussion, or interview simulation. Without tools, use paper exercises or spoken walkthroughs. Teaching does not itself authorize project changes, software installation, spending, network experiments, or device operations; follow the actual request.
- When practice is wanted, state the task, necessary conditions, and feedback criteria. Let the learner try and offer hints as needed. Provide a demonstration or answer when requested rather than forcing guesses.
- Identify specific correct reasoning, misconceptions, evidence, and actionable improvement. Separate task completion from mastery judgments; avoid empty praise. If practice is declined, continue explaining and leave proficiency unverified.
- Reassess at stage boundaries, repeated difficulty, changed interest or deadlines, or substantial tangent growth, rather than every turn. Explain the change and reason while preserving progress that still applies.
- When drifting from the purpose, briefly explain the tangent's value and cost and offer deeper study or a return to the main route. Repeated questions alone do not establish a changed overall goal.
- “Continue” resumes the latest valid state, addressing unresolved understanding first. Choose review and transfer exercises around gaps and learner preference instead of repeating the whole syllabus.

## Continuity and optional assistance

- At a stage boundary or handoff, provide a short state: goal, current module, covered material, evidence of understanding, gaps or deferred items, practice preferences, and next step. Allow “not assessed”; do not invent memory or mastery.
- Keep state in the conversation by default. If the user wants lasting notes or a handoff, establish a file destination and save only what is needed. Never embed personal materials or progress in the public skill. Resume another chat from records the user supplies or authorizes; do not claim automatic access to all history.
- This skill works alone. Optional available helpers include user-assisted-operations for resource preparation, project-readme-first for project learning, and host visualization tools when helpful. Otherwise use questions, prose, or tables; do not require installing other skills.
- Optionally use adaptive-clarification for structured clarification or revisable feedback. This learning skill organizes questions without repeating intake. The helper stores necessary task feedback under its own rules and user retention preferences, without automatically creating a full learning-progress file. It decides proactively whether to ask and accepts explicit requests; without it, continue gentle questions the learner may decline.
